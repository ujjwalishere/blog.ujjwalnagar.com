A debate on forest versus development

Posted on Aug 1, 2026 DRAFT

Response to a friend on Forests vs Development


Image: Screenshot of a post posted by a friend.

One of the dear friend of mine posted this today. This is not the first time I have seen such argument, even though I used to believe in it. If it were anybody else, I would have just skipped past it. Since he is a well-reasoned man, and a nice person, I feel he deserves some feedback. Also, almost 70% of the time I agree with his arguments, but I had my disagreements over time to time. I think we as a country ought to learn how to disagree, especially respectfully. Firstly, I would like to clarify that I do not refer to myself as an environmentalist or anything. I always hated these tags and never made peace with any tags imposed upon me. I consider myself a reasonable person, who can think for myself. I also want to say that the term environmentalism means different things to different people, and we will get to that. So, let’s get to the main arguments here:

  1. We cannot expect a country to develop, because infrastructure need to be built; Hence we need to clear significant amount of forests.
  2. Once a country is developed, they can restore forests.
  3. If we do not develop, we will remain trapped as a developing nation.

These are the three main arguments he is making here, as I can see. I would say they are good arguments and do have some merit. But I would also say how they also lead us into this zero-sum game type thinking. Let’s get to them one by one –

We cannot expect a country to develop, because infrastructure need to be built; Hence we need to clear significant number of forests.

Plainly I agree with the fact that we need to build infrastructure if we want to develop as a country. Any country, society need to build infrastructure, after all we all want to live in a house, get treated in a hospital, and travel to find work. But it all relies on this idea of development, and what we consider as development. GDP is the most used indicator of development, but I do not need to tell you how flawed it is a measure of development for any country. Even the Kuznets himself said that a nation’s welfare and quality of life cannot be inferred from GDP. Most of these measures of development are economic in nature and do not say anything at all about the actual development of a country. Development in my eyes in plain and simple terms should be about the welfare of people and better quality of life. No one should be living in poverty, and everyone deserves a decent lifestyle. These are synergistic with access to food, water, shelter, education but also to decent work and equality. So, we have to come up with better ways to measure development, and UN SDGs are one such attempt. There is whole debate on how we can better measure development.

Hence, the question of justice and equality matter the most, whose development are we talking about here. In case of India, the top 10% of the Indian population holds 77% of the total national wealth . Even if you talk about developed countries for e.g. US, such extreme inequalities are there. Some people like to plugin Trickle-down economics here, but that is has already been settled many times.

This makes that question of what infrastructure is more important. Do we really need to clear our forest to build, schools, hospitals, trains. Whom does the infrastructure serves, does it make people’s lives better? Do we need a 10-lane highway in the middle of forest, where trains can do the job. Apparently, the main reason for clearing forest is not even the availability of land to build things. Agricultural expansion is the biggest reason, which mostly goes to feed animals then to later feed on them. These problems have their solutions, one of which is how to better store food from getting wanted before it even reaches the plate. I would suggest to my friend to work on better food storage and transportation methods. Building infrastructure in India caused just 1.5% of forest loss , which itself can be avoided by better placement of infrastructure.

This assumption that we must clear forest to develop is inherently wrong. This does not need to be the case; it depends on the choices we make on how we want to get developed. https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7202948 Even the act of clearing forest goes against the idea of development. Even if you are just talking in economic terms, forests have a huge value. India’s forest alone provides around 2.5 trillion dollars’ worth of ecosystems services annually. This is obviously an underestimate because there is no way to, we can fully assess these services, because we do not understand them .

I am not even talking about the moral, social, environmental, costs that come from clearing the forest. The most vulnerable of society rely on these, and by clearing them we are making people less developed who need our so-called development. The communities who have relied on forest for centuries, and we are just snatching it away from them in the name of development. Can we calculate the value forest provided by storing the carbon, which would otherwise lead to certain degree of global warming with certain loss of GDP, we cannot.

Once a country is developed, they can restore forests.

His second argument has a flawed assumption, that we can grow forest back. This shows a lack of understanding about forests and what they are. I will say in simple clean terms, we cannot grow forests back. Forests are not just bunch of trees, they are ecosystems. You can have a hectare of bunch of trees, or you can have a hectare of forest. Just by planting trees you do not get the forests back. These forests are one of life’s most complicated and intricate relationships which we do not even understand. Many species of plants, insects, fungi, animals which have built these intricate relationships over centuries and millennia, you cannot build that. Some people argue that as science will evolve, we will be able to do that. My argument is you cannot build what you have not seen and understood. With the current extinction rate of species, we are headed for sixth mass extinction. We still do not know 86% of the species on earth. 110824-earths-species-8-7-million-biology-planet-animals-science

So many species will just disappear before we even will get the chance to discover them. Why does it matter here? Because of these species and their interactions, which make a forest. If we lose them, we lose forests. Why does it matter, because we depend on them, our food, our health, life itself depends on them. One of the classical examples that some scientists from UK were stealing (little sensationalized) from Sweden. Just read about it why? They are developed country, right? They can do anything now, apparently, even if you have tons of money, you cannot bring species back. People who claim otherwise are lying, even if you can; think about economics of it. If you still are not convinced, read about German forests most of which are planted forest.

When it comes to his third argument, I feel like I answered it in the first argument mostly. Yes we need to develop, but rethink what it actually means to develop. I would suggest my friend to read Amartya Sen’s Freedom as Development, even I have not read it completely but know the main argument.

In conclusion, This is not environmentalism; it is just common sense. Apart from all economics, forests also hold cultural and religious values. We never needed to become environmentalists because, culturally, we were told to preserve our environments. If you read about how environmentalism started, it did not start from India. It started when people felt the effects of destruction of forests. Even the economics of it became hard to ignore. It did not come out of our inherent altruism towards environment but from this realization that we and our economies depend on the services of forests and environment. Studies have shown that development and forests can go hand in hand, and many countries have already pledged that. So, I would only say to my dear friend to read a little more and realize that this is not our generation who is at fault, but our previous ones. We are only the victims of their approach towards development.
