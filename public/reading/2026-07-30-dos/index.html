Crime and Punishment

by Fyodor Dostoyevsky

It is an amazing novel, but hard to read unless you are an experienced reader in English. It was originally written by Dostoyevsky in Russian. One reason could be that it was written in 1866 in St. Petersburg, and I lack the social and cultural background behind it. There are always issues with translation, but I think the translator did an amazing job.

The novel follows the story of Raskolnikov, who came to justify murdering an old woman. He came up with this self-belief that it is justified to take extreme measures to do great deeds. In his case, justification was that by murdering the old pawnbroker lady, he could elevate himself and his family from poverty and can help many more people. He came to conclude that the death of one person can actually help many more people. What happened after he did his deed was a story of how extreme self-beliefs and radical ideas can lead gentlemen like Raskolnikov into doing evil acts.

When he came to the lady’s house and murdered the lady, he also ended up murdering her sister because she happened to arrive and saw what he did. He barely escapes being caught red-handed. After escaping from the house, he finds himself in a delirious and sick state. Even after convincing himself that he did a great deed, his moral qualms made him miserable and sick. He even forgot to steal what he came to steal in the first place, and whatever he did steal, he hid it somewhere. He just was not able to use the stolen money. The moral dilemma and the constant fear of getting caught made him insane.

He was a good man from the heart who even gave whatever money he received from his mother to a widow. Always a helpful person, it was hard for people to even believe that he could have done such a thing. He became sick and bedridden for days to come. He came to visit his friend Razumikhin, who helped him in his illness. His only family, his mother and sister, also came to meet him, but everyone was surprised by Roskolnikov’s behaviour. He tried to avoid everyone and became a self-contained and miserable man lost in himself. His reflections on what he did were eating him from inside, but he was not able to give up his beliefs. After struggling for a long time, he came to realise that admitting his crime and repentance is the only way to move forward in peace.

The story follows his journey from self-belief that any means are justified for great deeds to his realisation of what he did.

It was a long read but also a difficult one. One needs an understanding of the Russian context of 1800’s St. Petersburg, but anyone can feel the moral qualms a person might feel, given what he did. It was written in extreme detail, which made the whole scene lively in the head of the reader, but it can also feel slow and difficult to read. Overall, I enjoyed the book, but I will not lie; there were sometimes when I felt too slow and boring to read. I would say you need to persist to enjoy and get what Dostoevsky really wants to say. Do not judge this book by my rating; that only reflects how much I enjoyed it, which depends on a lot of things. I think it would change if I had more context into Russian society and history. But the amount of work and details the author has put in is commendable.
