My friends always see it as something that has to do with taking care of your trash. A lot of times they make fun of me, like “give us suggestion on how to handle this plastic wrapper”. They see the word sustainability and are like hmm, it is something about recycling, trash, etc. I’m not implying they are being rude; it's just their expression of what they know. It’s not just friends; this is a common narrative that I come across a lot of times. That is why I’ll try to unwind the whole thing.

Most of the time, I handle it calmly, but sometimes it annoys me. It seems like this word has kind of been hijacked by all the corporate advertisements. I have seen an explosion of ads like sustainable chocolate, sus home, housing society, etc. But do they really understand what it truly means and how this word came about? The literal meaning roughly says that something you can sustain over a period of time, in our case, across humanity.

If we are doing something and we can keep doing it indefenitely that process is sustainable. That is why using Oil as a fuel is not sustainable because we know that it is a limited resource and we’ll soon be done with it. Here most important thing to remember is the time scale. Technically, this universe itself is not sustainable; current theories suggest it will also eventually die in heat death. Funny isn’t it, we are trying to be sustainable in unsustaiable universe.

Jokes aside, but what matters is the time scale. On a human scale, 1000 years seems like a pretty big deal. This timescale is obviously a choice, but 1000 years should be enough to test, right?

So, if you can do something for 1000 years without running into problems, you are good to go. Here, you mean not you literally, but the humanity. If on a personal level, you are doing great but creating problems for your future grandkids, then think about it again. So, a lot of smart people scratched their heads and for the first time came to a single definition of The Sustainability—

It defines sustainable development as *“the development meeting the needs of today’s generation without compromising the well-being of future generations”.* 

This is the definition given by what was called as the [Brundtland Commission](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/139811). I know this referrs to to development because that is what we want as humanity. This definition covers all the activities of humanity as a whole, but obviously, you can downsample it to your trash example. 
Imagine your home as a whole planet, and you have a trash bin in your backyard. You thought you could keep pumping out trash, but one day you realise that the bin is full. Remember, you can’t throw this garbage anywhere because your home is the whole planet. Can you still keep producing trash? You have to use that trash and bring it back into the system if you want to keep going. If you can recycle your 100% of your trash, then it doesn’t matter how much garbage you produce. It matters because that is what is the most challenging task. 

Just like this example, this earth is our home, and there is no limit posed by this system, which is technically called the Earth System. I know this system is huge, it takes care of everything until humanity became so huge that we started to cross those limits which are defined by the Planetary boundaries framework. Six out of nine, which we have already breached. So we need to consious about what we are doing and how can bring back the trash into the system and stay under the boundaries. 

This matters for nations, for cities, industries, as well we as individuals. If our activities are such that we can not keep doing without harming our future generation, then that activity is not sustainable. If I am putting so much microplastics, pollution, and carbon into the Earth's system, then I’m harming myself and my future generation, hence I need to change. I need to change the way I do things, as a society, as a city, as well as an Individual.  

As a one-liner, “Sustainability is something that I can keep doing now without harming anyone in the future”
So if you feel you can keep eating chips and throwing away wrappers and you’re sure that your kids will not be sitting on a pile of plastics, go for it. If you think we can keep polluting our surroundings and your kids will be good, you’re good to go. If you think we as a humanity keep putting all these greenhouse gases into the atmosphere and you are sure the earth will not warm, then everything is great. Right?
But if things are the other way round, then we need to change the way we do things. We need to keep looking at the ways to do things differently until we find something that we are sure we can sustain, not until the heat death, but atleast a 1000 years.

This will require us as humanity to come together, but we as individuals also. Every small action matters, whether as an individual or as a collective. I know it is difficult, sometimes it may be impossible to do things individually, because we are part of this global system. There is a limit in terms of individual change, but there is no limit on how individuals can change systems. We built them, and obviously, if we have the will and want to, we can change them.

Disclaimer: This is not a guide to sustainability or anything; there are obviously much smarter people. Go read their amazing work. But I am pretty sure, you do not need a literature review to lead a more sustainable life.
