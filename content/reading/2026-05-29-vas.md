# I Love My Amma – Vasudhendra

![Vasudhendra](/images/vas.jpeg)

I am not an avid reader, but this one hooked me. I read the whole book in almost two sittings, which is a record. By the end of the first chapter, I was crying; It felt too personal. Vasudhendra’s story of his Amma somehow carried the universality, or maybe it was just me. It was so honest of Vasudhendra to admit in the introduction itself, how difficult it could be to write an autobiography and did not shy away that some stories might contain embellishments. Honestly in my opinion it does not matter, no stories can be as it as otherwise it will not be a story. I cried, rejoiced, and reflected upon the stories of my own Amma the whole time. How is she being no god but a human, doing her godly things.

Apart from amma, Vasudhendra’s life also represents thousands of stories from this country. Many students like me hails from similar background—tucked between the contrast of life back home and coming to a big city. All those experiences felt so real and personal, like somehow somebody has written down my future. Even if some words lost in translation, I was easily able to piece together what Vasu was trying to convey. I felt if you have had similar experiences in your life, sometimes only a hint is required. There were moment after moment, of Vasu’s life, even silly ones, which brought absolute joy when you look back at them. 

Now, I have finished reading the whole book, I still feel the urge to go over it again, I will sometime again. It also stirred me from inside reflecting upon my own amma. I felt I could see her more transparently now, understanding her empathy but also her decision. Yes! Mom can be annoying sometimes, but as Vasu rightly mentioned, you need get under the skin of that person, understand their fears, experience and then only you can judge their decisions. What matters is empathy, it does not matter if the decisions are right or wrong, this is something that stuck to me from one of the final chapters. I rightly agree with Vasu, it is difficult to teach empathy, but it can be caught.

“I love My Amma” is not a book, but a river, taking you on a journey to the most human world of all the Ammas. From the Kingdom of Steel to crazy world of bioscope of old days, it has it all.

Thank you Vaudhendra for sharing the story of Amma with us—this is another Shraddha to Amma and Appa from one more reader.
